Lesson 1 · 14 min

The seed and the garden: why AI is cultivated

Nobody writes, line by line, a model's ability to code or plan. The labs create the conditions and the abilities emerge. You do the same on a different scale: the model arrives ready-made, and what you cultivate is the environment around it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Why the model is the seed and doesn't change with use
  2. 2What you cultivate: the envelope around the model
  3. 3The cycle that turns errors into a better agent
  4. 4Where the garden metaphor breaks down

Goal: separate what the lab cultivates (the model) from what you cultivate (the agent) and explain the cycle that makes the agent improve.

  1. 1/7

    Process

    Everything starts with a real process: qualifying leads, organizing the week, scheduling appointments. Without a defined process, there's nothing to evaluate later.

  2. 2/7

    Agent

    The agent is the model (fixed, from the lab) plus the environment you wrote: role, context, tools, rules, examples, and memory.

  3. 3/7

    Execution

    The agent runs the task with the environment as it stands at that moment. This is where most people stop: they use it, like it or don't, and start over.

  4. 4/7

    Result

    What was delivered. It can look good and be wrong: fluent text isn't correct text.

  5. 5/7

    Evaluation

    Someone measures it: right, partial, or wrong, and why. Without evaluation, the agent repeats the same error with the same confidence.

  6. 6/7

    Feedback

    The evaluation becomes a concrete change in the environment: a new rule, an example, a limit. In the file, not in the conversation.

  7. 7/7

    What changes from one harvest cycle to the next

    It's not the model. It's what you noted about where it went wrong and what you adjusted in the environment. The first four steps make the agent work; evaluation and feedback make it improve.

Before, we programmed the behavior. Now, we program the process that produces the behavior.

02 · The two levels of cultivation

The point almost everyone mixes up.

1. Level 1

Training, weights, and scale happen at Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek. It costs billions and you don't take part.

The result is the seed: abilities that emerged from training, some expected, others that surprised even the people who trained it.

2. Level 2

The model arrives ready-made, with its weights frozen. What you cultivate is everything around it: role, context, tools, rules, examples, memory, evaluation, and feedback.

That envelope is what turns the same model into either a confused intern or a reliable professional.

ConsequenceIn use, the model doesn't learn on its own. When you say "my AI got better," what improved was the garden. And the garden is yours.

3. Software × cultivation

With traditional software, you write the exact rules and get exactly what was specified; an error is a bug on a single line.

With cultivated AI, you write the conditions and watch what emerges, and an error calls for a different environment: more context, another example, a new rule.

4. Your role

You don't need to be better than the AI at the task. You need to know how to create the environment where it produces the right result.

The work shifts from doing to defining the role, giving context, setting limits, evaluating, and giving feedback. It's people management, applied to systems.

04 · Where the metaphor breaks down

Read this before you get too excited.

1. It doesn't grow on its own

A plant grows on its own; an agent doesn't. Without evaluation and feedback, it repeats the same mistake forever.

2. It invents fruit

A plant doesn't invent fruit. An agent can produce a result that looks good and is wrong. That's why evaluation exists.

3. It takes work

Bad context, a bad example, and a vague rule produce a bad agent. The model is the same as your competitor's; the garden is not.

4. It's not a conversation

"Giving feedback" here means editing the environment, not chatting until it "understands."
